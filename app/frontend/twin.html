🔬 OPERATIONAL CALIBRATION: WHY >1.20 m/s² INSTEAD OF >10.5 m/s²?
ISO 10816 / UIC 518 / EN 14363 Standards
🟢 Baseline (<0.65 m/s² | <0.066 g)

Smooth Continuous Welded Rail (CWR): Normal rolling noise on healthy Singapore MRT track. Primary suspension isolates vibration effortlessly. Zero structural fatigue.

🟡 Moderate (0.65–1.20 m/s² | 0.07–0.12 g)

Dynamic Track Response: Mild acoustic roughness, sleeper passing transitions, or curve hunting. Passenger ride index remains compliant; no immediate track repair required.

🔴 Severe Corrugation (>1.20 m/s² | >0.12 g)

Abnormal Periodic Resonance: Sustained vibration triples baseline energy. Fastening clips fatigue, ballast pulverizes, and in-cabin noise exceeds 75 dBA. Triggers Level 2 rail grinding possession.

⚠️ Why not >10.5 m/s²? Standard gravity is g = 9.81 m/s². Sustained vibration of 10.5 m/s² (1.07 g) causes Wheel Unloading (Q ≤ 0) under UIC 518 where wheels literally lift off the rail head, causing acute derailment! Real-world corrugation wear (0.05–0.25 mm micro-ripples) produces 1.3 – 2.8 m/s² continuous RMS. If calibrated at >10.5 m/s², every damaged rail would falsely appear green until catastrophic failure.
👶 Simple 5-Year-Old Guide
What are we looking at? Think of this as a toy train with 64 wheels! 🟢 Green wheels are rolling super smoothly. 🟡 Yellow wheels feel small bumps on the track. 🔴 Red wheels are hitting rough, wavy metal ripples (called corrugation) that shake the train! Top row wheels ride on the Left Rail (Side I), and bottom row wheels ride on the Right Rail (Side II). If only one row turns red, we know exactly which rail is bumpy!

🤔 Why does the red warning turn on at 1.2 and not 10? Because 10 is as strong as Earth's gravity! If the train shook at 10, the wheels would literally float in the air and crash! The wavy ripples on the rails are tiny (thinner than a fingernail), so catching them early at 1.2 lets engineers grind them smooth weeks before they ever become dangerous.
